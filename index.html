The operational challenge

A claim should not depend on finding one sentence in a thousand-page file.

Accredited representatives may need to reconcile service records, medical evidence, examinations, decisions, correspondence, and lay statements before they can advise a claimant. Manual organization can consume the time that organizations need for consultation, development, and quality review.

⌕

Fragmented evidence

Relevant events may be separated across multiple files, document types, and decades.

↻

Repeated manual work

Each reviewer may rebuild the same chronology and evidence map from the beginning.

✓

Verification burden

AI summaries are only useful when representatives can quickly return to the original source page.

A reviewer-centered workflow

From claim file to structured human review

Claim Compass is intended to support accredited professionals—not replace their judgment or the systems used to establish representation and submit claims.

  1. 01

    Controlled intake

    Authorized organizational users add records within a defined case workspace.

  2. 02

    Document recognition

    Common military, medical, examination, decision, and correspondence records are classified.

  3. 03

    Evidence extraction

    Potentially relevant dates, diagnoses, symptoms, opinions, decisions, and events are surfaced.

  4. 04

    Condition organization

    Related evidence is assembled into timelines and condition-focused lifecycles.

  5. 05

    Representative verification

    The reviewer confirms, corrects, rejects, or annotates findings against the source record.

  6. 06

    Prepared consultation

    The organized record supports informed development, consultation, and quality-control decisions.

Responsible use by design

The representative remains the decision-maker.

Claim Compass may identify potentially relevant information, but accredited professionals determine whether the evidence is relevant, whether additional development is appropriate, and what advice is provided to the claimant.

What this concept does not do

  • It does not provide legal or medical advice.
  • It does not determine entitlement to benefits.
  • It does not guarantee a rating or claim outcome.
  • It does not replace VA systems or accredited representation.
  • It does not permit reliance on AI findings without source verification.
  • AI-assisted output may be incomplete, inaccurate, or omit relevant context.

Pilot partnership model

Evaluate the concept in a controlled organizational setting

A limited pilot can be structured around representative feedback, accuracy review, workflow fit, and measurable time savings before any broader implementation decision. Any pilot, licensing, data-processing, or service relationship requires a separate written agreement signed by KNECO, Inc. and the participating organization.

1

Define scope

Select participating representatives, approved use cases, and a limited evaluation period.

2

Configure access

Establish organization roles, reviewer assignments, and agreed data-handling boundaries.

3

Review performance

Measure source accuracy, usability, review time, and representative confidence.

4

Document findings

Produce recommendations for refinement, governance, and potential expansion.
